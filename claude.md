# CLAUDE.md

# BENGALURU — A LIVING GAME WORLD

You are building a beautiful, game-quality 3D social world set in Bengaluru.

This is NOT a 3D directory.
This is NOT a LinkedIn clone with 3D avatars.
This is NOT an AI-generated-looking city.
This is NOT a generic Three.js demo.

The goal is to create a world that feels like a real, polished video game.

People are represented as characters inside the world.
Their professional identity exists inside the game world.

Think:

"LinkedIn, but instead of scrolling through people, you walk through Bengaluru and discover them."

---

# 1. CORE PRODUCT IDEA

Users enter a stylized 3D Bengaluru.

Designers and builders exist inside the city as playable characters.

Each person can:

- Choose an avatar
- Customize their appearance
- Add their name
- Add their role
- Add their company
- Add their portfolio
- Add X / Twitter
- Add LinkedIn
- Add what they are currently building
- Mark themselves as "Open to Work"
- Choose where in Bengaluru they are located

Characters roam around the city.

Each character has a floating nameplate.

Users can:

- Walk around
- Explore the city
- Discover people
- Search for people
- Approach characters
- Open profiles
- Visit portfolios
- See what people are building

The city is the interface.
The people are the network.

# 7. CHARACTER READABILITY

Characters must work at three distances.

### FAR

The silhouette should be immediately readable.

### MEDIUM

The character's outfit and general identity should be recognizable.

### CLOSE

The character should have enough detail and personality to feel like a real game character.

Avoid tiny meaningless details.

Prioritize large shapes and strong silhouettes.

# 9. WORLD COMPOSITION

The environment should have strong visual hierarchy.

Every scene should have:

### FOREGROUND

The player and nearby characters.

### MIDGROUND

Streets, buildings, props and interactions.

### BACKGROUND

Bengaluru skyline, trees, buildings, atmosphere and landmarks.

Avoid visual noise.

Do not fill every empty space with random assets.

Negative space is allowed.

Every object should have a reason to exist.

---

Use cinematic stylized lighting.

- Afternoon

# 17. TECHNICAL PRINCIPLE

Prioritize visual quality over world size.

Do NOT build the entire Bengaluru immediately.

Start with a small, extremely polished district.

A tiny beautiful world is preferable to a huge unfinished world.

Performance matters.

The final experience should feel smooth and responsive.

Use level-of-detail strategies where appropriate.

Do not sacrifice the visual identity just to increase map size.

## ASSET PIPELINE

- Environment (buildings, roads, trees, street furniture): built in code from a hand-arranged kit of beveled pieces. No raw boxes.
- Characters: AI image-to-3D → Mixamo rig and animation → GLB. Characters are NEVER built from primitives.
- Hero props (auto, BMTC bus): AI image-to-3D → GLB, re-tuned to the palette.

Full rules are in design.md section 11.

# PHASE 5B — ONBOARDING + REAL PROFILES

Goal:

Let real designers put themselves in the city.

Principles:

- Explore first, ask later. Guests enter instantly as THE INVISIBLE (clothes, no body) and are never blocked by a form.
- "BECOME VISIBLE" (HUD button or V) opens a character-creation flow over the live street, never a web form:
  pick your look → who you are → what you're building → where people find you → neighbourhood + your spot in the district → go live.
- Your own nameplate floats above your character and updates live as you type.
- Saving uses Supabase (email magic link; no passwords). Draft survives the round trip in localStorage.
- New profiles start PENDING. You see yourself immediately; everyone else sees you once an admin sets status = approved.
- Row-level security: anyone can read approved profiles, people can only write their own, nobody can self-approve.
- Sample profiles retire automatically once enough real people are approved.

Schema: supabase/migrations/0001_profiles.sql

5. Is the lighting beautiful?
