# BENGALURU — SOUND DESIGN SYSTEM

Sound is a core part of the game.

The goal is not to add background music and generic UI clicks.

The goal is to make Bengaluru feel like a **living, premium game world**.

The sound design should have the level of intentionality, personality and polish found in high-quality character-driven games such as Overwatch.

Do not copy Overwatch's sounds, music, characters or audio assets.

Use the underlying principles:

- Strong sonic identity
- Extremely readable audio cues
- Characterful UI sounds
- Spatial audio
- Layered environments
- Dynamic music
- Audio feedback for every meaningful interaction
- Distinctive character sounds
- Strong audio transitions
- Audio that communicates information without requiring the player to look at the UI

---

# 01 — THE CORE AUDIO PHILOSOPHY

The city should sound alive even when the player is doing nothing.

The player should be able to close their eyes and still understand:

- Where they are
- Whether they are moving
- Whether someone is nearby
- Whether they discovered something
- Whether they entered a new area
- Whether something is interactive
- Whether they successfully performed an action

The soundscape should feel:

**PLAYFUL**
**PREMIUM**
**CINEMATIC**
**CHARACTERFUL**
**IMMERSIVE**
**RESPONSIVE**

Never generic.

---

# 02 — BENGALURU HAS ITS OWN SOUND

Do not use generic "city ambience."

Build a stylized Bengaluru soundscape.

Potential sound elements:

- Autos
- BMTC buses

- Horns
- Metro trains
- Metro announcements
- Pedestrian movement
- Café conversations
- Cutlery
- Street vendors
- Dogs
- Birds

- Rain
- Wind
- Trees

> BENGALURU AS INTERPRETED BY A GAME SOUND DESIGNER.

Real-world references should be recognizable, but the final soundscape should feel cohesive and stylized.

# 04 — SPATIAL AUDIO

Spatial audio is extremely important.

Sounds should exist in the world.

If a café is to the left:

The player should hear it from the left.

If a bus passes behind the player:

The sound should move:

LEFT → BEHIND → RIGHT

If the player walks away from a café:

The sound should gradually become quieter.

If the player walks behind a building:

Sound should become partially occluded or muffled where technically appropriate.

The environment should feel physically real even though the visuals are stylized.

---

# 05 — CHARACTER AUDIO

Characters should have personality.

Characters should not all sound identical.

Use subtle audio characteristics.

Examples:

ROBOT

- Mechanical clicks
- Servo movement
- Tiny electronic sounds

SUPERHERO

- Slightly heavier footsteps
- Cloth movement
- Subtle movement accents

DESIGNER

- Occasional laptop / typing sounds
- Backpack movement
- Subtle wearable/accessory sounds

BUILDER

- Tool / mechanical accents where appropriate

These should be subtle.

Never turn characters into cartoon sound machines.

---

# 06 — FOOTSTEPS

Footsteps should respond to the environment.

Different surfaces should sound different:

- Asphalt
- Concrete
- Grass
- Wood
- Indoor flooring
- Wet surfaces

Footstep sounds should match the character's movement speed.

Walking:

soft / controlled

Running:

faster / stronger

Stopping:

small natural deceleration sound

Avoid repetitive obvious loops.

Footsteps should feel natural and slightly varied.

---

# 07 — CHARACTER PRESENCE

A character should have a subtle sense of audio presence.

Not a sound constantly playing above their head.

Instead, use small cues when a player discovers or approaches them.

Example:

Player is walking through Koramangala.

Ambient city sounds continue.

A nearby resident becomes relevant.

A subtle:

`tink`

or:

`soft tonal pulse`

plays.

The player notices the person.

This sound should communicate:

> THERE IS SOMEONE INTERESTING NEARBY.

---

# 08 — DISCOVERY SOUND

Discovering a person should feel rewarding.

When a resident becomes highlighted or the player approaches an interesting character:

Use a short, memorable sonic cue.

It should be:

- Musical
- Warm
- Slightly playful
- Short
- Recognizable

Avoid generic:

`BEEP`

Avoid generic notification sounds.

This should become part of the game's sonic identity.

---

# 09 — INTERACTION AUDIO

Every meaningful interaction should have audio feedback.

Examples:

Hover:

`tck`

Selection:

`tik`

Interaction:

`soft mechanical click`

Profile opens:

`short musical impact`

Portfolio opens:

`clean transition sound`

Back:

`soft reverse movement`

Search:

`subtle scan`

Search result:

`small confirmation`

Find person:

`directional pulse`

The sounds should be fast and satisfying.

---

# 10 — PROFILE OPEN

When the player approaches:

SHAI

PRODUCT DESIGNER

[ E ] VIEW PROFILE

Pressing E should trigger a strong but short audio interaction.

Think:

**impact + soft tonal element + UI movement**

The sound should make the profile feel physical.

Avoid generic web notification sounds.

---

# 11 — SEARCH AUDIO

Searching for someone should feel like a game mechanic.

Example:

Player searches:

`Shai`

The interface performs a subtle sonic scan.

When the character is located:

**PULSE**

The camera begins navigating toward them.

The sound should guide the player toward discovery.

Eventually, directional audio could be used:

left → right

near → far

quiet → loud

The player should be able to understand the direction of the person through sound.

---

# 12 — UI SOUND LANGUAGE

The UI should have its own sonic identity.

Use a consistent family of sounds.

The UI sound palette should be:

- Clean
- Chunky
- Digital
- Warm
- Playful
- Slightly futuristic
- Tactile

Avoid generic application sounds.

The UI should feel like a game HUD.

---

# 13 — SOUND HIERARCHY

Sound should have a hierarchy.

### LEVEL 1 — WORLD

Traffic, wind, people, birds, vehicles, weather.

### LEVEL 2 — CHARACTER

Footsteps, movement, subtle personality sounds.

### LEVEL 3 — INTERACTION

Nearby people, interactive objects, discoveries.

### LEVEL 4 — UI

Buttons, menus, search, profile interactions.

### LEVEL 5 — MAJOR EVENTS

Events, important discoveries, location transitions.

The mix should prevent all layers from competing.

---

# 14 — DYNAMIC MUSIC

Do NOT use one looping background track.

Music should be layered.

Base:

Very subtle environmental musical texture.

Exploration:

Light melodic layer.

Discovery:

Additional musical layer.

High-density area:

More rhythmic energy.

Night:

Different instrumentation / atmosphere.

Rain:

More atmospheric and spacious.

Event:

Unique musical theme.

The music should respond to what the player is doing.

---

# 15 — MUSIC STYLE

The soundtrack should feel like an original game soundtrack.

Possible direction:

- Modern electronic
- Organic percussion
- Warm synths
- Playful melodic motifs
- Subtle Indian rhythmic influence
- Atmospheric textures
- Carefully designed transitions

Do NOT make:

"generic Indian lo-fi."

Do NOT use stereotypical tabla/sitar elements simply to signal India.

Bengaluru's identity should come primarily from the **world soundscape**, while the music establishes the game's own universe.

---

# 16 — LOCATION TRANSITIONS

Moving between neighborhoods should have subtle audio transitions.

Example:

Koramangala → Indiranagar

The ambient layers gradually change.

Do not suddenly cut from one soundscape to another.

Use crossfades and transitional sounds.

The player should subconsciously feel:

> "I'm somewhere different now."

# 19 — EVENTS

Events should have their own sonic identity.

Example:

A design meetup begins.

The area gradually gains:

- Crowd ambience
- Music
- Conversations
- Event sounds
- Announcements

The player should be able to hear that something is happening before seeing it.

---

# 20 — SILENCE MATTERS

Do not fill every moment with sound.

Quiet areas should exist.

Use silence and low-volume ambience to create contrast.

A park at night should not sound like Koramangala traffic.

A quiet residential street should not sound like a nightclub.

Silence is part of the design.

---

# 21 — NO AUDIO SLOP

Never add a sound simply because:

"Games have sound effects."

Every sound needs a purpose.

Avoid:

- Generic notification sounds
- Stock game explosions
- Random whooshes
- Constant ambient loops
- Excessive UI clicks
- Generic futuristic beeps
- Unnecessary bass hits
- Constant music
- Random character noises
- Overly loud effects

The soundscape should feel authored.

---

# 22 — AUDIO CONSISTENCY

All sounds should feel like they belong to one game.

Before adding a new sound ask:

> Would this sound exist in the same game as every other sound?

The answer must be yes.

Build a coherent sonic vocabulary.

Reuse motifs intelligently.

Do not create every sound independently.

---

# 23 — THE OVERWATCH PRINCIPLE

Take inspiration from the underlying philosophy of high-quality character-driven multiplayer games:

Every interaction should have:

- Clarity
- Personality
- Timing
- Impact
- Emotional feedback

A player should know what happened from sound alone.

But do NOT copy any existing game's audio.

Create an original Bengaluru-specific sonic language.

---

# 24 — SIGNATURE AUDIO MOMENT

The game should eventually have a recognizable moment that belongs uniquely to this product.

Example:

A player is walking through Bengaluru.

They hear normal city ambience.

A subtle discovery tone plays.

They turn.

A character is highlighted.

The music briefly changes.

The nameplate appears:

SHAI
PRODUCT DESIGNER
BUILDING DESIGNFOLIO

The player approaches.

[E] VIEW PROFILE

A satisfying interaction sound plays.

The profile opens.

This entire sequence should feel like:

## DISCOVERING A PERSON IN A GAME WORLD.

That is the emotional experience we are designing.

---

# 25 — FINAL AUDIO TEST

Before shipping any audio feature, ask:

1. Does this communicate something?
2. Does it have personality?
3. Does it fit the Bengaluru soundscape?
4. Does it fit the game's visual identity?
5. Does it feel spatial?
6. Does it make interaction satisfying?
7. Is it subtle enough?
8. Is it memorable?
9. Does it sound handcrafted?
10. Does it make the world feel more alive?

If not:

REDESIGN THE SOUND.
